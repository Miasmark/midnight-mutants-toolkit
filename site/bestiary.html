Palette 6 has no fixed value — it is rewritten every frame. f6:$4C70, in the per-frame path, does 
JSR Random
STA P6C1        ; a random colour byte
EOR #$F0        ; hue nibble flipped, luma kept
STA P6C2
ADC #$10        ; one hue step further
STA P6C3

The three entries are always c, c^$F0 and (c^$F0)+$10 — hue-contrasted at matched luminance rather than three unrelated colours, which is what makes it strobe rather than flicker as noise. sub_4329 forces any part into palette 6 while its flash flag is set, so that is what everything turns while being hit, and the bulb-head zombie is authored to use it permanently. The cycle above is a real sequence of sixteen frames generated by that formula; the game's own values differ each time because the seed is the running random state.

One bignose bobs, two do not. Three kinds — 0, 1 and 4 — draw the same bignose head. Only kind 0 gets the per-frame vertical offset at f6:$641A; the others are skipped by a PLA / BNE on the kind. The difference is in the draw code, not in the artwork, so it is invisible in a sprite sheet.
Orange is hard to render, and that is not a modern problem. On NTSC hardware of this period orange sits very close to brown, and the two are separated as much by chroma artifacting on a composite signal as by the palette entry. A flat arithmetic conversion of a colour byte will always read duller than a CRT does, which is part of why the sampled values here are closer to the game's intent than the numbers alone.
The pumpkin-head is coloured from a screenshot. Palette 2's middle entry in the base block is $02, a flat grey, which is why the head rendered grey while the game plainly shows orange. That palette is taken from a frame of play instead and marked colour observed on the card. Palette 7 — head and torso for most kinds — is sourced properly, rewritten per kind by sub_67A3 from dat_67E9.
The walk cycle is four frames per facing. A zombie's body sprite is rebuilt every frame at f6:$638F as base | (facing + counter&3), so each direction has four body frames while the head stays put. Playback is a CSS steps() animation over a single strip, so it freezes on the first frame under reduced motion.
The bob is a sine table. Kind 0 alone takes a per-frame vertical offset at f6:$641A, indexing dat_DD00 by frame_ctr × 16 and shifting right five. That table sweeps smoothly from $00 to $FF and back, so the offset traces a 16-frame wave from −2 to +5 pixels. The bignose kinds run side by side above: kind 0 rides the wave, kinds 1 and 4 hold still.
Colour comes from two places. Each part of a figure carries its own MARIA palette index in the top three bits of its palette-and-width byte — typically 7 for the head and torso, 5 for the legs. Palettes 0-6 are loaded as one 33-byte block from b3:$81C9, while palette 7 is rewritten per kind by sub_67A3 from dat_67E9. So a type's identity colour is its palette 7, shown as swatches on each card, and the legs are the same brown on everything because they come from a shared palette.

The luma nibble scales with a gamma and no floor on the dark end. An earlier version lifted every colour by a constant so luma 0 would not be pure black, which turned the two darkest entries of every palette into muddy olive — the bat's wings are luma 1 and 0 and are plainly black in play, against the gold stripe that is the luma 6 entry of the same palette.

Colours are converted with a per-hue base table rather than a phase formula. The earlier converter placed hue 9 in the olive range when it is blue, which was obvious the moment a screenshot of a zombie was put beside it.
Three damage rules, not one. Every weapon is a projectile, and what a hit removes depends on what it strikes:
targetdamage per hit
most actors, including this castweapon_level — so the knife, at level 0, never kills
ghostsweapon_level + 1 — SEC / ADC weapon_level at f6:$625F
bosses1 flat, unless it is the knife

The ghost rule is visible in the collision loop at f6:$623B. The ordinary rule is corroborated arithmetically: a spawn seeded $98 needs 8 steps to reach death, and takes 8, 4 and 3 hits from axe, blaster and mega blaster — exactly weapon_level per hit. The projectile-versus-special-actor site itself is still unlocated, so the numbers above rest on that rule rather than on a line of code.

These are ordinary actors, not special ones. They live in the eight-slot array based at $8F with actor_mode ($AF) as both state and health, and they die on reaching $A0. Bats, crows and wolves are seeded $FF or $FE — already past that threshold — so any hit kills them, the knife's zero damage included. The spider is seeded $98, eight points short, which is why it survives eight axe blows and the knife never finishes it. That is the enemy reported in play as indestructible.
The ghost is a third system again. It has its own two slots at ghost0_active ($1EB3), is seeded $E0 for 32 points, and is drawn from the area's sactor_def3 budget rather than the roaming array. It is also the only cast the knife can kill: the ghost collision at f6:$625F does SEC / ADC weapon_level, so a level-0 knife still lands 1 damage a hit where every other target takes weapon_level and therefore nothing.
They draw from one frame table, not from a per-creature set. dat_5CE2 is four bytes and dat_5E6A twenty, selected by facing. The bat is 2 bytes wide and the crow 4, straight from the palette-and-width byte each path writes. All of them use MARIA palette 5 — the same shared palette as the zombies' legs.

Dying

The head is thrown; the body comes apart.

Each frame is four 8-pixel pieces — two torso halves and two leg halves — and the offsets in the record grow frame by frame, so the body comes apart and the pieces fly outward. dat_6641 is indexed by a counter that decrements, so the play order is $60 through $65, left to right above. The first three frames use the zombie's own torso and leg cells split down the middle; the last two swap in the fragments at $E4–$EE on page $E0.

The last frame's right leg really is the item reticle — confirmed in play. This looks like a rendering error and is not one. The record is explicit, and the reticle is visible on screen in the final frame of a zombie's death, though the flashing palette makes it easy to miss: 
$65 -> $49EA
E0                page $E0
FE F1 E8 FB       2 bytes, gfx $E8, offset -15,-5
FE 19 EA FB       2 bytes, gfx $EA, offset +25,-5
BE F2 EC 0E       2 bytes, gfx $EC, offset -14,+14
BE 16 EE 0E       2 bytes, gfx $EE, offset +22,+14
00                end

The fourth piece is $EE at the right-leg position, and $EE-$EF is the same eight pixels the Grampa screen draws as the item-select reticle. The artwork is shared between two unrelated purposes, which is ordinary in a ROM this tightly packed — the zombies already borrow the player's legs, and the humpback's body records live in a different bank from everyone else's. What is unusual here is that the reuse is visible: the reticle is a UI element appearing, briefly, as part of a creature's corpse.

That is the melting stretch. Page $E0 from $E0 to the left half of $EC was long the last unidentified region of the sprite sheet, read from play as a melting death shared with the wolf. The composite records since settled it: records $40–$4E draw the wolf and name $E0–$EA for its death, and the special actors' own death records $64/$65 borrow $E4–$EE from the same run — which is how the reticle at $EE ends up on a corpse. It is reached from the generic special-actor death path, so every zombie melts through the same frames as the wolf.

Meanwhile the head is thrown clear: sub_63BF places it at pos_cross + $10 − 2 × sactor_damage, so it rises as the counter runs out, and its facing is taken from dat_63E1 = $00 $08 $04 $0C indexed by damage & 3 — it tumbles through all four directions on the way up.

How the types are laid out

Not one sprite per creature — parts, shared and interleaved.

Every zombie is a head from page $C0 over legs that belong to the player's own walk cycle. That is why the page holds both: the legs are drawn once and re-used, and a type is really just a set of heads.

The stride is 16, not 4. sub_63F5 shifts sactor_facing twice, but that field is already stored multiplied by four (f6:$6314), so the real index is direction × 16 + kind and the table is four rows of sixteen. Reading it with a stride of four produces a sliding window that mixes facings together, and an earlier version of this page did exactly that — it showed four kinds where there are thirteen.

The humpback is the exception in shape: its cells are full 16-pixel width because it supplies its own torso rather than borrowing the player's, which is why it is drawn twice as wide above and has no shared-legs panel.

The other heads sit in $A0–$BC as two cells per facing, four halves each, in a fixed order — bignose, a spare slot, longneck, hunchback. Three types use all four facings. The spare slot is recycled for the types that need fewer: the headless and bulb-head zombies have a single head that serves every direction, and the pumpkin-head has two. The humpback, the lab-pair and the Dr Evil zombies live higher up, at $E0 and above, with four facings apiece.
